Topological domains in mammalian genomes identified by analysis of chromatin interactions

Jesse R. Dixon, Siddarth Selvaraj, Feng Yue, Audrey Kim, Yan Li, Yin Shen, Ming Hu, Jun S. Liu & Bing Ren

Ludwig Institute for Cancer Research, University of California, San Diego · Department of Statistics, Harvard University

Introduction

The spatial organization of the genome is intimately linked to its biological function, yet our understanding of higher order genomic structure is coarse, fragmented and incomplete. Here we investigate the three-dimensional organization of the human and mouse genomes in embryonic stem cells and terminally differentiated cell types at unprecedented resolution.

At bin sizes less than 100 kilobases, highly self-interacting regions begin to emerge. These regions, which we term topological domains, are bounded by narrow segments where chromatin interactions appear to end abruptly.

To identify systematically all such topological domains in the genome, we devised a directionality index to quantify the degree of upstream or downstream interaction bias for a genomic region.

Results

Genome-wide maps reveal topological domains

The domains defined by the model were reproducible between replicates. Interaction frequencies within domains were consistently higher than interactions spanning domain boundaries.

These findings are best explained by a model of genomic DNA organized into spatial modules linked by short chromatin segments. The organization remains stable across cell types while interactions inside each domain can remain dynamic.
